## Axioms

### 1. Ik Onkar (One God)

There is one formless, eternal creator. The Mool Mantar opens the Guru Granth Sahib with this declaration.

**Example**: "Ik Onkar, Sat Naam, Karta Purakh, Nirbhau, Nirvair, Akal Murat, Ajuni, Saibhang, Gur Prasad" — One God, True Name, Creator Being, Without Fear, Without Hate, Timeless Form, Beyond Birth, Self-Existent, By Guru's Grace. This IS the D — everything derives from these attributes.

---

### 2. Guru Granth Sahib as Living Guru

After the ten human Gurus, Guru Gobind Singh (1708) declared the scripture itself as eternal Guru.

**Example**: The Guru Granth Sahib is not merely a text — it IS the Guru. It is installed on a throne (manji sahib) in every gurdwara, attended to, put to rest at night, awakened each morning. A book that IS a person = unique in world religion.

---

### 3. Ten Gurus (Temporal Chain)

Sikh revelation unfolds through ten human Gurus (1469-1708), each building on the previous.

**Example**: Guru Nanak (founder, 1469) → ... → Guru Gobind Singh (tenth, 1708). Each Guru added something: Guru Angad standardized Gurmukhi script, Guru Arjan compiled the Adi Granth, Guru Gobind Singh created the Khalsa. The temporal chain IS progressive revelation.

---

### 4. Khalsa (The Pure)

The Khalsa brotherhood, created by Guru Gobind Singh in 1699, defines the relational boundary of baptized Sikhs.

**Example**: Amrit Sanchar (baptism ceremony) — initiates drink amrit from the same bowl, take the names Singh (lion) or Kaur (princess), adopt the Five Ks (kesh, kangha, kara, kachera, kirpan). The Khalsa IS the community boundary — visible, embodied, egalitarian.

---

### 5. Three Pillars of Practice

Naam Japna (meditation on God's name), Kirat Karni (honest work), Vand Chakna (sharing with others).

**Example**: The langar (community kitchen) at every gurdwara. Everyone sits on the floor, everyone eats the same food, regardless of caste, class, or creed. This IS operational governance — equality enforced through shared meals.

---

### 6. Akal Takht (Throne of the Timeless)

The supreme seat of Sikh temporal authority, established by Guru Hargobind (sixth Guru).

**Example**: Five takhts (seats of authority) govern Sikh institutions worldwide. The Jathedar of Akal Takht can issue hukamnamas (edicts) binding on the entire Khalsa. This IS structural governance — centralized temporal authority alongside the spiritual authority of the Guru Granth Sahib.
